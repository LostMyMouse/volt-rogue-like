# Comic opening, Vanguard and Fortress rooms

The superhero team is **The Vanguard**, led by **Halo**. Volt joins later and is absent from the established-team spread.

The intro opens on an illustrated VOLT #1 cover, held for 4.2 seconds with a soft appearance. The cover turns over 1.8 seconds to a seven-second Vanguard rescue spread. Turning its page begins Halo's introduction, followed by Bastion, Echo, Thorn and Patch. A villain spread appears after Patch and before Kilnheart. Subsequent character sequences turn their pages over 1.6 seconds. Automatic scene changes retain the existing narration-completion gate; Next turns pages manually and Skip exits immediately. The spreads are illustrated establishing pages; the individual action sequences retain their authored motion and panel camera.

Four short, character-specific speech bubbles accompany each action sequence. They are unvoiced comic typography; the story's existing recorded Volt narration remains verbatim. Bubbles scale and stay within the action viewport on desktop, small landscape phones and portrait screens. Printed speaker names clarify who is speaking.

The Fortress painting now has an open bunk passage with the obstructing cupboard removed, and a real armoury door. Floor collision and armoury prompts follow these openings. The armoury is a separately painted room in the same Fortress style, with Patch's repair bench, training rug, weapon alcoves and a walkable exit to the hall. It has a shared illustrated robot trainer for practice and tutorial. Tutorial camera framing includes stationary dummies as well as parry targets. Restart in practice resets practice without beginning or counting a run; tutorial Restart restarts training. Return works from the button and through the bottom doorway. Existing weapon/variant unlocking costs remain in effect.

Dialogue output is 35% quieter and effects 45% quieter at the same slider settings, including existing saves. Sliders remain independent and music mix is preserved.

Rooftop crates use four unmarked illustrated storage props. They sit beside rooftop machinery and parapets, at least 130 world pixels from jump/landing points, outside fixed prop collision. Loot is rolled once per crate: 42% credits (12–20), 8% health pack, 50% empty. Contents do not change the visible prop. Breaking a crate awards no kill; credits and health appear as physical pickups, and each crate pays at most once.

Ordinary rooms use the same generic rooftop, without district colour grading or district-specific HUD labels. Bosses use their bespoke arenas. Only the final Redactor approach uses the tower theme before its boss. Glasswake's boss arena is now an illustrated hall of mirrors. Refraction begins in phase one, places exactly two destructible doubles plus the real boss in shuffled safe positions, and preserves the solid-white versus broken-ring identity cue. Killing a double cancels its beam and removes 15 Composure. Clearing both creates a 1.1-second opening; striking the real boss during refraction creates a 1.7-second opening and clears the doubles. Telegraphed beam damage and the existing lunge/parry rules remain intact.
